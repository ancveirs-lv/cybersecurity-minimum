# Cybersecurity Minimum — detailed guide

## C01 · Start important actions yourself

If a message or link asks you to sign in, pay, confirm identity or enter sensitive data, do not continue from the supplied link. Open the official app or site yourself.

## C02 · Verify the payment recipient

Before paying, verify the recipient, amount and purpose. Treat unexpected changes to bank details or payment instructions as a reason to verify independently.

## C03 · Urgency means pause

Pressure, threats and “act now” language are common social-engineering tools. Slow down before doing anything consequential.

## C04 · Verify unusual requests another way

If a colleague, bank, manager, friend or relative unexpectedly asks for money, codes or sensitive data, verify through a separate trusted channel.

## C05 · A message does not prove who sent it

Names, logos, caller IDs, email addresses and profile images can be spoofed or copied. Verify identity independently when the request matters.

## C06 · When in doubt, verify first

Uncertainty is a reason to stop and check, not a reason to take a risk.

## C07 · Never share passwords, PINs or verification codes

Do not send, dictate or enter passwords, PINs or one-time verification codes because another person tells you to.

## C08 · Use a unique password for every account

Password reuse lets one breach threaten many accounts.

## C09 · Use a long passphrase or generated password

Prefer long, unique and unpredictable passwords. A password manager can generate them; if you must remember one, use a long passphrase not based on personal facts.

## C10 · Store passwords safely

Use a password manager instead of unprotected notes, documents, email drafts or contact fields.

## C11 · Use passkeys or multi-factor authentication

Use a passkey or other phishing-resistant method where available. Otherwise enable multi-factor or two-step verification.

## C12 · Protect your primary email account

Your primary email is often the recovery path for other accounts. Give it stronger authentication and secure recovery settings.

## C13 · Review important account security settings

Periodically review sign-in methods, recovery information, active sessions and security notifications.

## C14 · Remove old devices and unnecessary access

Revoke sessions, devices and connected applications you no longer use or recognize.

## C15 · Lock phones and computers

Use a strong device PIN/password or biometrics and enable automatic locking.

## C16 · Keep devices and software updated

Install security updates promptly and enable automatic updates where practical.

## C17 · Keep built-in protection enabled

Keep supported anti-malware, firewall and platform security protections enabled and current. Install mobile apps from trusted official stores.

## C18 · Secure home Wi-Fi and the router

Change default administrator credentials, keep router firmware current, use modern Wi-Fi security such as WPA3 where supported, and disable unnecessary remote administration.

## C19 · Make backups you can restore

Keep important data in separate backups and periodically test that recovery actually works.

## C20 · Consider protective DNS or equivalent network filtering

A reputable protective DNS or equivalent network-level service can block access to known malicious domains. Treat it as an additional control, not a guarantee.

## C21 · Treat unexpected attachments and QR codes with caution

Unexpected attachments, links and QR codes can lead to malware or phishing. Verify the sender and context before opening them.

## C22 · Do not install remote-access software because a stranger tells you to

Scammers may ask you to install remote-control or “security” software. Do not hand control of your device to an unsolicited caller or message sender.

## C23 · Polished design is not proof of trust

A fraudulent website, profile or advertisement can look professional. Verify the domain, source and whether you initiated the action.

## C24 · Be cautious with too-good-to-be-true offers and emotional stories

Scams often use extraordinary investment returns, inheritance stories, romance or emergencies to create trust and pressure before asking for money or data.

## C25 · Report and respond quickly

Stop the activity, preserve relevant evidence, secure affected accounts and contact the relevant service provider or financial institution. Report the incident to the appropriate national cyber authority or law-enforcement channel where applicable.
